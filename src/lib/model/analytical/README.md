# Analytical calculations

Place verified, framework-independent model formulas here. Do not infer a
result's assumptions from unrelated diagnostics.

## Safe boundary

`safe-boundary.ts` implements the manuscript's safe-level lemma, equation
`eq:safe-boundary`, in `sections/2_model_verification.tex` of the separate
research project. The stable finite-endpoint expression was also checked
against `code/utils/+policy/safeWealth.m`. Those sources were read, not run or
modified; the manuscript remains a research draft and has not undergone formal
mathematical review. The website has no runtime dependency on the project.

Inputs are annual continuously compounded real risk-free rate `r`, constant
annual real withdrawal `C`, impoverishment threshold `xImp` in euros, elapsed
time `t` and the remaining-lifetime support endpoint `T`, both measured in
years after retirement. The result is in euros. The implemented domain is
`r > 0`, `xImp > 0`, `C > r * xImp`, `0 <= t <= T`, with `T > 0` or infinity.
At finite `T`, the value at `t = T` is the continuous limit; the lemma states
the boundary for `t < T`. For an unbounded endpoint the boundary is `C / r`.

This is a standalone calculation and is not connected to the evaluation panel.
It requires a known withdrawal and the actual mortality support endpoint; the
last age in the empirical cohort table is not itself a fitted endpoint. It
does not establish availability of the paper's policy or feasibility results.
The tests use hand-computable finite/infinite cases, limit and monotonicity
checks, and invalid-input and numerical-stability cases. No MATLAB run is claimed.

## Safe withdrawal and compatible CE policy

`safe-withdrawal.ts` implements the inverse risk-free withdrawal relation,
equation `eq:safe-withdrawal-rate` in the same manuscript section. It requires
`initialCapital > xImp > 0`, `r > 0` and a strictly positive finite endpoint or
infinity; the result is in real euros per year. It uses `expm1` for short
horizons and is tested by inversion against the safe boundary.

`compatible-policy.ts` implements the compatible CE results in
`sections/4_compactified_policy.tex`: the compatible power value function and
feedback (`eq:compactified-value`, `eq:compactified-feedback`), sustainable
withdrawal and required capital (`eq:sustainable-withdrawal-compactified`,
`eq:required-capital-compactified`), and global simplex feasibility
(`eq:simplex-slackness`). The corresponding policy-construction logic was
checked against `code/utils/+policy/buildPolicySpecification.m`, read only.

The policy functions require a finite CE endpoint and check the mortality
parameters against the supplied market's real rate and positive `rho`. They
accept the calibrated Merton direction in its existing asset order, with
negative components allowed for the unconstrained policy. Callers must supply
parameters from a valid, converged fit and a valid calibrated market; the
functions check compatibility but do not establish fit provenance or convergence.
Input and output monetary amounts are real euros, and rates and probabilities
are decimal fractions. These are CE-model results, not GM-model or projected
policy results. The feedback is zero outside the risky region as stated in the
theorem; at the finite endpoint it is undefined.

The global feasibility diagnostic reports a negative Merton component separately
from borrowing. When all components are nonnegative, its `relativeBandwidthExcess`
is the manuscript's zeta (the ratio to the maximum bandwidth); feasibility is
tested using `zeta <= 1`. The calculation uses ordinary floating-point
comparisons; results extremely close to one should be interpreted accordingly.
It does not calculate borrowing probability or expected borrowing time.

Tests include a hand-computable example, risk/withdrawal/capital round trips,
boundary and negative-component cases, rejection of incompatible inputs and a
real fit generated by the website's CE fitting code. No new MATLAB execution
or independent formal verification of the manuscript is claimed.
